# Groww FirstStep: Product Brief

## The User Problem
First-time Gen Z investors in India (aged 20-26) often feel intimidated by the complex jargon, market volatility, and pressure to make immediate choices when opening their first investment account. They lack contextual knowledge on how much they should actually invest given their existing financial obligations, leading to either poor financial decisions (investing emergency funds) or complete inaction.

## Target Audience
Indian youth aged 20–26, including college students, part-time workers, and young professionals receiving their first paychecks. They have limited financial literacy but a high intent to start wealth creation.

## Key Assumptions
- Users are willing to provide basic financial estimates (income, expenses) if they see immediate value (a personalized readiness check).
- Educational content presented contextually (during mock investing) is more effective than separate, long-form articles.
- Reducing the pressure to execute a real transaction builds trust and increases long-term retention.
- A clean, uncluttered interface reduces cognitive load and anxiety for beginners.

## The Most Important User Journey
1. **Onboarding:** User completes a skippable "Financial Comfort Check" to estimate their investable amount without using sensitive data.
2. **Dashboard:** User lands on a beginner-friendly dashboard highlighting learning and preparedness over immediate investing.
3. **Learn & Simulate:** User interacts with an SIP simulator to understand compounding, takes a short quiz to test their knowledge, and sets a mock financial goal.
4. **Mock Discovery:** User explores generic investment categories in a risk-free environment, learning about risks and horizons before ever committing real money.

## Value Proposition
"Learn to invest at your own pace, without the pressure of real money. Discover how much you can comfortably invest and build your financial foundation before taking the leap."

## Core Product Hypothesis
"If first-time investors can understand how much money they can comfortably invest, learn the essentials in context, and take an informed next step without pressure, they will be more likely to complete their first meaningful investing journey."

## MVP Scope
- **In Scope:** Financial comfort check onboarding, beginner dashboard, educational learning center with a quiz, interactive SIP simulator, mock goal tracking, mock investment discovery, and local data persistence.
- **Out of Scope:** Real brokerage APIs, real transactions, KYC (PAN/Aadhaar) collection, live market data, complex technical charting, personalized financial advice.

## Success Metrics & Guardrails
- **Success Metrics:**
  - Onboarding completion rate (target >80%).
  - SIP simulator interaction rate (target >60%).
  - Beginner quiz completion and average score.
  - Goal creation rate.
- **Guardrails:**
  - Users misinterpreting illustrative returns as guaranteed (measured via comprehension quiz).
  - Users feeling pressured to invest immediately (measured via qualitative feedback).
  - Users confusing mock investments with real portfolio holdings.

## Principal Risks and Trade-offs
- **Risk:** Users might skip the educational content if it feels too much like homework.
  - *Mitigation:* Keep lessons short, interactive, and directly tied to practical tools like the SIP simulator.
- **Trade-off:** By not asking for real financial data or KYC, the personalization is limited.
  - *Rationale:* Trust is the primary constraint for Gen Z. Lowering the barrier to entry is more important for a first step than high precision.
